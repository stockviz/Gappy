# 02 — Momentum crash prediction

Daniel and Moskowitz (2016), *Momentum Crashes*, motivates this study. The paper links winner-minus-loser (WML) crashes to bear markets, high market variance, and rebounds led by the loser portfolio. The local summary is at `../../summaries/momentum/Momentum Crashes (2016).md`. This is an exploratory India/US adaptation, not a replication of the paper's CRSP deciles or its GJR-GARCH dynamic strategy.

## What the paper concludes about WML

WML buys stocks with strong returns over roughly the preceding year (excluding the latest month) and shorts those with weak returns. It earns positive returns on average in the paper's samples, but occasionally suffers large, persistent losses. The worst crashes tend to follow market declines when volatility is high. As the market rebounds, distressed past losers can rise much faster than past winners; the short loser leg then drives WML's loss. The authors describe that exposure as resembling a short call on the market.

Those conditions are partly observable before a crash. In the paper, a strategy that adjusts WML exposure using forecasts of both its expected return and its variance roughly doubles the static strategy's alpha and Sharpe ratio. Volatility scaling alone does not capture the expected-return change. These are the paper's findings, not results established by this India/US adaptation; our simpler overlays do not reproduce its dynamic model.

The India implementation trades equal-weight top/bottom-20 single-stock futures, ranked on adjusted cash-price 12-2 momentum. Its ranking universe is rebuilt at every decision close from futures with an actual quote for the contract about to be held; the build checks daily quoted holdings on both legs and fails on material missing coverage. The first run omitted that filter and is invalid; see `india/README.md` for the correction and audit. The US implementation uses equal-weight top/bottom deciles of point-in-time S&P 500 constituents ranked on raw, unadjusted 12-2 price momentum. Both form positions at a close and apply them from the next trading day. They charge turnover costs and select any overlay parameters using data through December 31, 2019 only. PRE ends that day; POST begins May 1, 2020. The January–April 2020 gap is excluded from parameter fitting but included in FULL.

Start with `findings.md` for the cross-market comparison. The market reports and reproducible scripts are in `india/` and `us/`. Each arm writes a checkpoint, daily returns, holding/feature and exposure audits, pre/post/full metrics, cumulative-plus-drawdown charts, and color-coded metric tables. The artifacts are exploratory: the US raw closes omit dividends and corporate actions, and the India PRE sample has too few bear days for a reliable bear-by-variance mean forecast.

Run from each market directory: `Rscript test-helpers.R` (India) or `Rscript tests/test_helpers.R` (US), then `Rscript build.R` and `Rscript analyze.R`. After an India build, also run `Rscript verify-quotes.R` to cross-check every selected futures position against historical source quotes. The scripts source the validated R2 common returns and chart helpers; database access is needed for the builds and the India quote audit.
