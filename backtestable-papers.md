# Papers that can be backtested with the current StockViz data

Prepared from the summaries in `/mnt/data/books/Gappy/summaries` and the existing studies under `/mnt/ssd1/stockviz/R2/backtests`.

This is a feasibility catalogue, not a claim that every paper can be reproduced exactly. `Direct` means the core signal and required price history are already available in the current data estate. `Adaptation` means the paper's idea can be tested, but the original universe, market, frequency, or implementation must change. `Conditional` means the data exists in principle, but the study needs an additional extraction, audit, or more careful instrument construction.

## Data available for these studies

- Indian daily index total-return and price series in `bhav_index`, including NIFTY 50 TR, MIDCAP 150 TR, MIDCAP SELECT TR, SMALLCAP 250 TR, and other index series.
- Indian cash-equity adjusted OHLC in `StockVizDyn.eod_adjusted_nse`, with `RETURN_SERIES_ALL` as a fallback for some return calculations.
- Indian single-stock and index futures in `BHAV_EQ_FUT`, with contract-safe roll helpers already used by the futures studies.
- MCX commodity futures in `BHAV_COM_MCX` / `bhav_com_mcx`.
- NSE momentum signals: `MOMENTUM_ABS`, `MOMENTUM_PROB`, and related scores at 50-, 100-, and 365-day lookbacks.
- Free-float market capitalization, price-band eligibility, corporate actions, information-discreteness scores, earnings-date disclosures, and index valuation fields.
- India VIX history, VIX futures, daily index OHLC, and intraday NSE index, option, and MCX futures bars.
- US daily equity/ETF prices, historical S&P 500 constituents, US momentum signals, and CBOE VIX futures.
- Binance hourly crypto history for BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT.

The house protocol should be applied to all new work: causal one-day signal lag, chronological selection, pre-period selection and post-period holdout, explicit costs, contract-safe futures returns, and separate pre/post/full reporting. In the current R2 convention, named PRE ends on 2019-12-31 and named POST begins on 2020-05-01.

## Papers that should not be promoted to the first backtest queue

The following families are not directly supported by the current data without importing new point-in-time datasets:

- Fundamental value, profitability, accrual, investment, book-to-market, earnings-surprise, and analyst-forecast papers when the required historical fundamentals are not already materialized. Examples include Sloan accruals, Novy-Marx gross profitability, Fama-French value, and post-earnings-announcement drift. Earnings dates alone are not earnings surprises.
- International country/industry allocation papers that require historical country-level total returns and investable constituent universes. Indian industry work is possible, but it is an adaptation rather than a global replication.
- Credit-risk, CDS, Treasury-basis, and fixed-income papers requiring bond curves, CDS quotes, default histories, inventories, or funding spreads not present in the current database.
- Market-impact, limit-order-book, and execution-optimality papers unless the intraday bars contain the required depth, trade, quote, and order-book fields. One-minute OHLC bars are not a limit-order book.
- Full option-pricing and volatility-surface studies until option-chain coverage, rates, dividends, contract multipliers, and executable prices pass a dedicated audit.

## Recommended first queue

1. Time-series momentum and volatility scaling across NIFTY TR indices, MCX futures, and crypto.
2. Momentum crash prediction using the existing drawdown, crowding, IV, beta, and HMM diagnostics.
3. Universal/online portfolio selection versus 1/N, risk parity, maximum diversification, and volatility targeting.
4. Drawdown-based and semi-Markov exposure control, evaluated with episode-level significance.
5. Commodity futures trend, roll-yield, diversification, and volatility-cycle tests.
6. Short-term reversal and pairs/mean-reversion studies on a point-in-time Indian equity universe.
7. A separate option-data audit before attempting the index-futures option papers.

The queue deliberately starts with mechanisms that can be tested using existing data and engines. It avoids spending time on papers whose headline result depends on unavailable fundamentals, fund-level holdings, or unverified option-chain history.
