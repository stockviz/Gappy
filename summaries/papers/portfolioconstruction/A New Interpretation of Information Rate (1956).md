# 1. Metadata

- **Title:** A New Interpretation of Information Rate
- **Author(s):** J. L. Kelly Jr.
- **Year:** 1956
- **Journal/Venue:** *Bell System Technical Journal*

# 2. Problem statement

The paper asks a foundational question linking information and capital growth: **if an investor repeatedly bets on an uncertain outcome using side information from a communication channel, what maximum exponential growth rate of wealth can be achieved, and how is it related to the channel’s information rate?**

# 3. Approach (short)

The method is information-theoretic optimization of expected log wealth. Kelly formulates repeated betting as a multiplicative wealth process, chooses betting fractions conditional on a received signal, and maximizes the expected logarithmic growth rate. In the fair-odds case, the maximized growth rate equals the mutual information between the underlying event and the received signal.

# 4. Approach (detailed)

1. **Betting setup**

   Let $X$ denote the true outcome and $Y$ the side information. Conditional on $Y=y$, the bettor chooses fractions $b_x(y)$ of wealth with
   $$
   b_x(y)\ge 0,\qquad \sum_x b_x(y)=1.
   $$
   If the odds on outcome $x$ are $o_x$, wealth is multiplied by $b_x(Y)o_x$ when $X=x$.

2. **Growth-rate objective**

   Over repeated independent rounds, the almost-sure exponential growth rate equals the expected log multiplier:
   $$
   G(b)
   =
   \sum_y p(y)\sum_x p(x|y)\log\big(b_x(y)o_x\big).
   $$
   This is the quantity Kelly maximizes.

3. **Optimal bet fractions**

   For fixed $y$, the maximization problem is
   $$
   \max_{\{b_x\}} \sum_x p(x|y)\log b_x
   \qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad
   \sum_x b_x = 1.
   $$
   The Lagrangian is
   $$
   \mathcal L = \sum_x p(x|y)\log b_x + \lambda_y\Big(1-\sum_x b_x\Big).
   $$
   FOCs give
   $$
   \frac{p(x|y)}{b_x(y)}=\lambda_y
   \quad\Rightarrow\quad
   b_x^\star(y)=p(x|y).
   $$
   Thus one should bet in proportion to posterior probabilities.

4. **Fair-odds case**

   Under fair odds $o_x = 1/p(x)$, the maximum growth rate becomes
   $$
   G^\star
   =
   \sum_{x,y} p(x,y)\log \frac{p(x|y)}{p(x)}
   =
   I(X;Y),
   $$
   the mutual information. This is the paper’s headline theorem: information rate is the maximum capital-growth rate generated by using the side information optimally.

5. **General odds**

   With arbitrary odds, the same optimization yields the same posterior-proportional stakes, but the growth rate decomposes into an information term plus an odds term. The fair-odds equality to mutual information is the special clean case.

6. **Proof logic**

   The proof is a direct constrained maximization plus the law of large numbers for repeated multiplicative wealth. The information-theoretic identity arises after substituting $b_x^\star(y)=p(x|y)$ into the objective and recognizing the mutual-information sum.

7. **Portfolio interpretation**

   In later finance language, Kelly’s criterion chooses the portfolio that maximizes expected log wealth growth. The paper itself is written for betting and communication channels, but the mathematical structure is exactly the ancestor of log-optimal portfolio theory.

# 5. Domain of applicability

- The result applies to **repeated multiplicative gambles or investments** with side information.
- The exact equality to mutual information requires the fair-odds benchmark.
- The paper is not a general portfolio model with covariance matrices or incomplete markets; those developments came later.
- Its enduring contribution is the exact link between **log-growth optimality** and **information**.
