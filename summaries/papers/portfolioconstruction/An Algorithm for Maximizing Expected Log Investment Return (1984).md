# 1. Metadata

- **Title:** An Algorithm for Maximizing Expected Log Investment Return
- **Author(s):** Thomas M. Cover
- **Year:** 1984
- **Journal/Venue:** *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*

# 2. Problem statement

The paper studies the optimization problem
$$
\max_{b\in\Delta_m} W(b), \qquad W(b):=\mathbb E[\log(b^\top X)],
$$
where $X\in\mathbb R_+^m$ is a random vector of asset gross returns and $\Delta_m=\{b\ge 0,\ \mathbf 1^\top b=1\}$ is the simplex of portfolio weights. The question is: **is there a simple iterative algorithm that converges to the log-optimal portfolio and admits monotonic improvement guarantees?**

# 3. Approach (short)

The method is concave optimization with a multiplicative fixed-point update. Cover maps a current portfolio $b$ to the expected post-period composition induced by market returns,
$$
b_i'=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{b_iX_i}{b^\top X}\right],
$$
and proves that this update monotonically improves expected log return, with one-step gain lower-bounded by a Kullback-Leibler divergence. The analysis blends information geometry and portfolio theory.

# 4. Approach (detailed)

1. **Define the log-optimal objective**

   Let $X=(X_1,\dots,X_m)\ge 0$ be the random market vector with known distribution. For $b\in\Delta_m$,
   $$
   W(b)=\mathbb E[\log(b^\top X)].
   $$
   Because $\log$ is concave and $b^\top X$ is linear in $b$, $W$ is concave on the simplex. Hence an optimizer $b^\star$ exists.

2. **Introduce the update map**

   Starting from $b$, define the next iterate $b'=T(b)$ componentwise by
   $$
   b_i'=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{b_iX_i}{b^\top X}\right], \qquad i=1,\dots,m.
   $$
   Since
   $$
   \sum_{i=1}^m b_i' = \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\sum_i b_iX_i}{b^\top X}\right]=1,
   $$
   and each component is nonnegative, $b'\in\Delta_m$.

   Economically, $b_iX_i/(b^\top X)$ is the fraction of end-of-period wealth in asset $i$ if one starts the period at $b$. Thus $b'$ is the expected ex post composition.

3. **Prove monotonicity**

   The core theorem is
   $$
   W(b')-W(b)\ge D(b'\|b)\ge 0,
   $$
   where
   $$
   D(b'\|b)=\sum_{i=1}^m b_i'\log\frac{b_i'}{b_i}.
   $$
   Hence each step increases $W$, and the increase is strict unless $b'=b$.

   A proof sketch: by concavity of $\log$,
   $$
   \log\frac{b'^\top X}{b^\top X}
   =
   \log\sum_i \frac{b_i' b^\top X}{b_i}\cdot \frac{b_iX_i}{b^\top X}
   \ge
   \sum_i \frac{b_iX_i}{b^\top X}\log\frac{b_i'}{b_i}.
   $$
   Taking expectations gives
   $$
   W(b')-W(b)\ge \sum_i b_i' \log\frac{b_i'}{b_i}=D(b'\|b).
   $$
   This is exact and is the paper’s main information-theoretic inequality.

4. **Characterize fixed points**

   If $b'=b$, then the KL lower bound is zero and no improvement is possible. Cover shows such fixed points satisfy the Kuhn-Tucker conditions for optimality. Intuitively, at a fixed point the expected post-return composition equals the starting portfolio, which is precisely the self-consistency condition for the log-optimal allocation.

5. **Monotonicity of the ratio to the optimum**

   A second theorem establishes monotonic improvement in relative terms: the ratio of the current suboptimality to the optimal value shrinks along iterations. This supplements the one-step gain bound and supports global convergence.

6. **Convergence**

   With strictly positive starting portfolio $b^{(0)}>0$, the sequence
   $$
   b^{(n+1)}=T(b^{(n)})
   $$
   has accumulation points, and every accumulation point is optimal. Since $W(b^{(n)})$ is monotone increasing and bounded above by $W^\star$, the values converge. Cover’s convergence theorem then identifies the limit set with the set of maximizers. Under uniqueness of $b^\star$, the whole sequence converges to $b^\star$.

7. **Upper bound on the optimum**

   The paper also gives an upper bound for the gap between current and optimal value in terms of the current portfolio and the gradient-like quantities generated by the update map. This provides a stopping criterion: once the bound is small, the current portfolio is near optimal.

8. **Relation to short-run competitive optimality**

   Cover notes that the same log-optimal portfolio also appears in one-period competitive games where payoff depends monotonically on relative wealth. That does not change the algorithm, but it broadens the interpretation: the optimizer is not only asymptotically growth optimal but also game-theoretically natural.

# 5. Domain of applicability

The algorithm applies to static one-period log-optimal allocation when the return distribution of $X$ is known well enough to compute the expectations in the update. It is exact for long-only weights on the simplex and a log objective. The argument is distribution-free beyond existence of the relevant expectations. Its practical limitation is that the update itself still requires evaluating $\mathbb E[b_iX_i/(b^\top X)]$, which may be analytically or computationally hard in large models. The paper also treats the unconstrained simplex problem; transaction costs, leverage constraints, and dynamic learning are outside its formal scope.
